# 1 · Research question and scope

## Primary question

How might university examination evaluation become **more explainable, consistent and reviewable** while preserving academic judgment, discipline-specific nuance, institutional autonomy and the practical capacity of examiners?

A meaningful assessment dispute raises more questions than whether a mark could be changed:

- Was the approved learning outcome clear to students and examiners?
- Did the criterion reasonably capture different correct or defensible responses?
- Can the institution reconstruct the original marking decision from preserved evidence?
- Was marking consistent enough for the consequential use of the score?
- Is there a defined, timely and impartial route for correction or review?
- Would a proposed change improve fairness without making the whole process slower or unaffordable?

## Research objectives

1. Compare alternative ways to publish or communicate paper-specific marking expectations.
2. Explore reasonable treatment of partial credit and alternative correct methods.
3. Study the institutional design of moderation and quality assurance.
4. Trace student evidence-access and review pathways, distinguishing different kinds of errors.
5. Specify auditable, privacy-aware requirements for digital evaluation.
6. Develop an **evaluable** pilot proposal rather than asserting benefits in advance.

## Scope distinctions

PU-ETF is a **proposed framework for university evaluation**, not an examination board, an automated marking service, an institutional complaint, or a mechanism for securing a predetermined higher grade.

An individual academic dispute can motivate a question, but it is **not a representative data set** for institutional policy. The project must be assessed against legal authority, faculty judgment, implementation costs and evidence across relevant assessment types.
