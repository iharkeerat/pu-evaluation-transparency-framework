# 4 · Examiner moderation and calibration

## Research problem

How could institutions detect inconsistent marking **without** assuming that differences between examiners are automatically errors or undermining legitimate academic discretion?

### Proposed questions for study

1. Would exemplars and a brief pre-marking calibration exercise improve agreement?
2. When is dual marking or sample-based moderation proportionate to the stakes?
3. How can drift across long marking sessions be monitored while accounting for changing response quality?
4. How should workload, anonymity, conflicts of interest and reviewer competence be managed?
5. What level of discrepancy should trigger discussion, a second evaluation or broader quality checks?

### Candidate methods (not results)

- Anonymous sample scripts assessed independently by qualified markers.
- Agreement measures suited to the **scale and structure** of the marks, accompanied by raw disagreement distributions.
- Review of discrepant scripts and a written adjudication rule.
- Workload and time-cost analysis alongside quality measures.
- Pre-specified criteria for escalation and safe stopping.

A numerical agreement score alone is not proof of validity, and a well-calibrated group of examiners may still share the same flawed rubric. Independent curricular and accessibility review are complementary safeguards.

**Evidence status:** conceptual evaluation plan only; no inter-rater dataset or institutional partnership represented.
